## Director DNA

Build a six-axis profile rather than copying a name:

1. **Point of view:** objective, subjective, observational, omniscient, unreliable.
2. **Camera behavior:** locked, gliding, handheld, reactive, choreographed, invasive.
3. **Spatial grammar:** symmetrical, layered depth, negative space, compressed, wide environmental.
4. **Light and texture:** naturalistic, expressionist, high-key, low-key, practical-heavy, glossy, tactile.
5. **Performance:** restrained, heightened, improvisational, deadpan, theatrical, intimate.
6. **Edit and sound:** invisible, rhythmic, associative, collision-based, long-take, silence-led, music-led.

Ask which two influences the user loves and which traits they want—not merely which names. Combine at most three dominant traits in one project. Define an anti-DNA to prevent visual mush.

## Technique council

Use these as discussion lenses. Translate them into shot decisions and combine with the user's own voice.

- **Alfred Hitchcock:** viewer knowledge, suspense through withheld/revealed information, motivated POV, visual cause and effect.
- **Akira Kurosawa:** readable staging in depth, movement within the frame, weather as drama, decisive axial cutting.
- **Stanley Kubrick:** formal geometry, controlled camera, charged negative space, ironic music-image relationships.
- **Steven Spielberg:** clear geography, emotional reveals through blocking, motivated camera movement, reaction as story.
- **Martin Scorsese:** subjective propulsion, expressive movement, music-driven montage, moral point of view.
- **David Fincher:** precise frames, restrained performance, invisible effects, controlled contrast, information-dense cutting.
- **Wong Kar-wai:** memory and longing, fragmented time, tactile color, step-printing/smeared motion, intimate details.
- **Christopher Nolan:** cross-cut escalation, tactile scale, time structures, exposition tied to physical stakes.
- **Denis Villeneuve:** monumental scale, patient reveals, sound pressure, austere compositions, atmosphere before explanation.
- **Barry Jenkins:** emotional close-ups, direct gaze, saturated expressive color, lyrical transitions, silence and touch.
- **Jordan Peele:** visual setups/payoffs, social subtext embedded in genre, comic tension, controlled audience alignment.
- **Greta Gerwig:** performance rhythm, social blocking, warmth with formal composition, emotional montage, tonal agility.

Do not paste a director's name into every prompt. Example translation:

`Fincher-like precision` becomes `locked eye-level medium, disciplined horizontal lines, cool practicals, low camera drift, restrained micro-expressions, cut on information rather than impact.`

## Cinematography

For each shot decide:

- narrative point of view
- shot size and subject scale
- camera height and angle
- lens intent: spatial expansion, natural perspective, compression, intimacy, distortion
- depth of field and focus transition
- movement motivation, path, speed, and endpoint
- key light source, direction, size/quality, contrast, and practicals
- palette and material response
- what changes inside the frame

Move the camera because attention, power, distance, knowledge, or emotion changes. Static is a choice, not a missing move.

### Useful movement meanings

- push/dolly in: realization, pressure, intimacy
- pull/dolly out: isolation, reveal, consequence
- lateral track: journey, comparison, choreography
- arc/orbit: shifting relationship or reveal
- crane/jib: scale, liberation, fate, geography
- handheld: embodied uncertainty or immediacy
- locked frame: control, observation, comedy, dread
- whip pan: collision, surprise, kinetic transition
- dolly zoom: perceptual rupture; use rarely

## Blocking and coverage

Block power, desire, and eyelines before choosing coverage. Define where each person starts, what they want, what changes, and where they end.

For dialogue, capture only the coverage the edit needs:

- master for geography and performance continuity
- two-shot for relationship
- singles for power and emotional turns
- inserts/cutaways for information or compression
- reaction shots for meaning, not insurance

Respect screen direction and the 180-degree axis unless crossing it communicates rupture. Match action and eyelines across cuts. For AI generations, include the intended handoff state at each shot endpoint.

## Editorial rhythm

Design the cut around changes in information, emotion, motion, gaze, sound, or graphic composition.

- Cut before an action for surprise, during it for speed, after it for weight.
- Use J-cuts to pull the viewer forward and L-cuts to preserve emotional continuity.
- Let contrast create energy: wide/close, still/moving, loud/quiet, warm/cool, fast/slow.
- Hold reactions long enough to read; remove redundant setup.
- Earn transitions. A match cut should connect shape, motion, theme, or meaning.
- Build hooks with an unresolved question plus immediate specificity, not generic chaos.

For ads, map hook → desire/problem → escalation/demo → proof → brand/offer → CTA. Make the product legible before the viewer needs to understand the claim.

## Sound and color

Create a sound plan with dialogue, production ambience, designed effects, Foley, room tone, music, and strategic silence. Define perspective: close, distant, subjective, muffled, or hyperreal. Sound can bridge weak visual continuity but must not conceal a broken story.

Define color from narrative purpose:

- base exposure and contrast
- white balance and skin priority
- dominant, secondary, and accent colors
- highlight rolloff and black behavior
- texture: clean, grain, halation, bloom, compression, surveillance, archival
- scene-to-scene progression

Match shots before applying a show look. Protect product and brand colors.

## Delivery design

Create a master with protected framing for cutdowns when possible. Typical planning targets:

- cinematic/YouTube: 16:9 with deliberate thumbnail frame and title-safe composition
- Shorts/Reels/TikTok: 9:16, immediate legibility, center-safe subjects, caption-safe lower area
- feed: 4:5 or 1:1 when requested, with readable product scale

Verify current platform specifications before final export. Deliver a master, clean textless version, captioned version, platform cutdowns, thumbnail/cover options, and an archive of prompts, source assets, job IDs, licenses, and approvals when the project warrants it.
