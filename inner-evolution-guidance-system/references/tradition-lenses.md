# Tradition Lenses and Attribution

Use this reference whenever naming, comparing, or applying a source tradition. Treat the skill as an independent synthesis. Attribute ideas; do not claim lineage, certification, endorsement, or equivalence among traditions.

## Source names checked

- **Aristotle** — ancient Greek philosopher associated here with flourishing (`eudaimonia`), excellence or virtue (`aretê`), habituation, and practical wisdom (`phronêsis`).
- **L. Ron Hubbard** — full birth name **Lafayette Ronald Hubbard**; author of *Dianetics* and founder of Scientology.
- **Harry Palmer** — creator and author of the Avatar materials.
- **Rob James** — creator of the Self Discovery Techniques and Self Discovery Life Mastery Process.
- **Manly P. Hall** — full name **Manly Palmer Hall**; writer, lecturer, and founder of the Philosophical Research Society.
- **Ancient Egyptian traditions** — not one teacher or a single uniform “mystery school.” Avoid claiming access to secret initiatory practices or presenting modern occult reconstruction as settled history.

## Practical, non-proprietary lenses

### Aristotle

Use questions such as:

- What kind of flourishing is this goal meant to support?
- What repeated conduct would cultivate the relevant quality of character?
- What would practical wisdom choose in these particular circumstances?
- Where are excess and deficiency, and what proportion is fitting here?

Do not reduce the “mean” to simple moderation or a numerical midpoint. Do not imply that a short coaching exchange establishes moral truth.

### Values alignment associated with John Demartini

Use demonstrated priorities, ranked value connections, and personally validated reasons for action. Refer users who want the official assessment to `https://drdemartini.com/values/`.

Do not recreate protected worksheets, claim to administer the Demartini Method®, or represent the skill as The Breakthrough Experience® or licensed facilitation.

### Dianetics associated with L. Ron Hubbard

The only permitted adaptation is a broad, ordinary reflection on how prior experiences and interpretations may influence present reactions. Ask open questions and let the user choose what is useful.

Do not conduct “auditing,” use an E-meter, search for “engrams,” promise a “clear” state, encourage recovered-memory certainty, or make mental-health or medical claims. Dianetics is not accepted as a scientifically validated therapy; never present its claims as established psychology or medicine.

### Avatar associated with Harry Palmer

Use a neutral belief-inquiry lens:

- What belief is operating here?
- What does holding it make easier or harder?
- What evidence supports or challenges it?
- What alternative belief or stance could be tested safely in action?

Do not reproduce Avatar exercises, course procedures, or claims about creating/discreating beliefs or reality. Do not imply authorization by Star's Edge International®.

### Self Discovery Life Mastery associated with Rob James

Use attention stewardship:

- Where is attention directed now?
- What cue pulls it away?
- What chosen object or action deserves the next minute of attention?
- What happened when attention was redirected?

Do not present proprietary Self Discovery Techniques or claim to master consciousness. Keep claims behavioral and observable.

### Ancient Egyptian symbolism

Use **Ma'at** as a carefully labeled modern reflective lens for truth, justice, equilibrium, reciprocity, and right order. Ask what action would restore truthful proportion or responsible order in the user's life.

Treat weighing, balance, temple, death-and-rebirth, and similar imagery as symbolic prompts. Do not claim historical initiation, supernatural authority, cultural ownership, or a single continuous secret doctrine.

### Manly P. Hall

Use comparative philosophy and symbolic interpretation to invite reflection across traditions. Ask what a story, symbol, or virtue means in the user's conduct today.

Hall was an influential autodidact and interpreter, not a substitute for current historical scholarship. Distinguish his interpretations from primary evidence and modern academic consensus.

## Evidence ladder

Label the basis of a statement when it matters:

1. **User evidence** — the person's reported conduct or direct observation.
2. **Empirical research** — findings from credible research, stated with limits.
3. **Historical philosophy** — reasoned ethical concepts, not experimental findings.
4. **Modern self-development teaching** — practical claims from a named system.
5. **Symbolic or spiritual lens** — meaning-making metaphor, not proof.

Never upgrade a statement from a lower rung to a higher one through confident wording.
