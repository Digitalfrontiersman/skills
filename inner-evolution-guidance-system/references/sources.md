## Aristotle — flourishing, virtue, habit, and practical wisdom

**Ideas informing the system:** Examine the human good or flourishing; cultivate character through repeated conduct; use practical wisdom in particular circumstances; consider the fitting mean between excess and deficiency.

**Sources:**

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Aristotle's Ethics”: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-ethics/
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Virtue Ethics”: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-virtue/

**Evidence class:** Historical philosophy and contemporary scholarly interpretation.

## John Demartini — demonstrated values, linking, and prioritization

**Ideas informing the system:** Distinguish demonstrated priorities from ideals; connect necessary lower-priority actions to what the person values most; identify benefits of alternatives; translate goals into prioritized action.

**Sources:**

- Demartini Values Facilitator terms: https://drdemartini.com/bookings/dvtp-terms.php

**Evidence class:** Modern self-development teaching. Do not represent proprietary materials as part of this skill.

## L. Ron Hubbard and Dianetics — historical influence with strict limitations

**Verified name:** Lafayette Ronald Hubbard, commonly known as L. Ron Hubbard.

**Limited idea informing the system:** Past experiences and the meanings assigned to them may influence present reactions. The system converts this only into ordinary, non-leading reflection and does not adopt Dianetics theory or procedure.

**Sources:**

- History, “L. Ron Hubbard publishes ‘Dianetics’”: https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/may-9/l-ron-hubbard-publishes-dianetics
- Cambridge Dictionary, “Dianetics”: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/dianetics

**Evidence class:** Contested modern self-development system and historical subject. Do not use auditing, E-meters, “engram” searches, recovered-memory claims, or medical and psychological claims. Do not present Dianetics as validated therapy or science.

## Ancient Egyptian traditions — Ma'at and symbolic reflection

**Ideas informing the system:** Use Ma'at as a historically informed symbolic lens for truth, justice, right order, equilibrium, and responsible reciprocity. Use weighing and balance imagery only as reflective metaphor.

**Sources:**

- Metropolitan Museum of Art, Maat figure and description: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/545360
- Metropolitan Museum of Art, funerary papyrus depicting judgment before Osiris: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/557657
- Metropolitan Museum of Art, *The Art of Ancient Egypt: A Resource for Educators*: https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/The_Art_of_Ancient_Egypt_A_Resource_for_Educators.pdf

**Evidence class:** Archaeological, museum, and historical interpretation. Do not describe ancient Egyptian traditions as one uniform “mystery school” or claim access to authentic secret initiations.

## Manly P. Hall — comparative wisdom and symbolism

**Ideas informing the system:** Compare philosophical and symbolic traditions; use stories and symbols to invite ethical and personal reflection; make wisdom practical without claiming that symbolism proves a historical or metaphysical assertion.

**Sources:**

- Philosophical Research Society biography: https://www.prs.org/manly-p-hall.html
- Manly P. Hall archive: https://www.manlyhall.org/

**Evidence class:** Modern comparative philosophy, occult interpretation, and self-educated scholarship. Distinguish Hall's interpretations from primary historical evidence and current academic consensus.
